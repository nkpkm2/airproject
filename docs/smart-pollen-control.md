# Pollen-aware smart control

## Purpose and operating boundary

The feature in the family overview is an opt-in advisory/actuation controller for Cupressaceae pollen (including cypress/Chinese arborvitae). It is paused by default. It only issues a physical power-button pulse or one mode-button pulse at a time after the device has a passed four-channel light calibration, an explicit weak-pollen baseline, and a fresh online state. Manual commands remain independent. Devices with unknown/contradictory lamps, no calibration, hidden catalog entries, or a stale heartbeat are skipped.

The current public pollen APIs have country/plant coverage limits: Open-Meteo's CAMS pollen fields are listed only for European pollen forecasts, while Google's country table lists China as grass-only and Japan as Japanese cedar/cypress. Neither can be treated as a Cupressaceae measurement in China. Weather is therefore only bounded contextual evidence. The operator can separately enter a current official level (fresh for 48 hours) and a future level with an explicit start/end window (review fresh for up to seven days), plus a source URL. When the URL is HTTPS on a `*.gov.cn` domain, the local service also tries once per hour to read a small HTML page. It follows only government-domain HTTPS redirects, caps response size at 500 KB, and accepts only fresh pages with an explicit publication date, a Cupressaceae term, and a recognizable level/peak statement. Date-only peak statements are represented as a whole local calendar-day window starting at 00:00, not an invented 10:00 event; the configured preboost lead begins before that boundary, while the final forecast date remains inclusive through that day's end. Approximate旬 forecasts likewise begin at the first estimated calendar day and are explicitly labeled. This is an estimate, not an hourly pollen observation. The accepted level forecast is normally limited to 48 hours from publication; a freshly refetched article that explicitly names an active multi-day peak window may remain usable through that window plus 12 hours, subject to a strict seven-day publication-age ceiling. Ambiguous, expired, unrelated, or unavailable pages remain visible as errors and do not override the operator setting. This is deliberately not a general-purpose scraper. Public Open-Meteo weather is fetched every 15 minutes after coordinates are configured and is attributed in the UI.

Social-data integration is intentionally not an unauthenticated scraper. The [Baidu Index help page](https://index.baidu.com/Helper) says the public edition does not support downloads and does not offer an open API; its [current homepage](https://index.baidu.com/v2/index.html?lang=zh) separately advertises API keyword-add/data-interface products, but the available scope, export rights, and price require account-side verification, so this controller does not assume that offering is a usable allergy-trend feed. The [Xiaohongshu scopes](https://openaccount.xiaohongshu.com/docs/scope) currently list only `basic_info` as open; `read_notes` remains planned, so that API cannot provide allergy-note search. The [Weibo Open Platform CLI](https://open.weibo.com/cli/quickstart) currently advertises search and trend capabilities, but requires developer verification, OAuth authorization, and an activated seven-day trial or paid credit service; read endpoints and quotas still need to be confirmed against the specific account's enabled command catalog before integration. No account has been authorized and no paid service enabled for this household tool. Therefore social/allergy discussion remains a bounded, operator-verified input (up to 18 points, enough only to reach the normal tier by itself, with 12-hour expiry) until an account owner explicitly authorizes a compliant, read-only connector; it must never be mistaken for a species-specific pollen measurement.

## Inputs and score

The controller calculates an explainable 0–100 score:

- Current official/local Cupressaceae level is primary: level 0/1/2/3/4 starts at 0/12/30/52/72 points. A manually entered future forecast has separate level, start/end window, and review timestamp; days before the lead window it is explanatory only, during the lead window it raises the target by one tier, and inside its declared window it contributes its full level. Current level and future forecast are never conflated.
- Current/forecast weather can change the score by at most 12 points, and only with a present-day pollen context: the configured local pollen season, a valid current official level, or a fresh reviewed symptom trend. A future peak bulletin alone does not activate today's weather score; it stays in the forecast lane until its explicit preboost/peak window. Weather alone cannot activate risk outside a current pollen context. Current rain uses Open-Meteo's backward-looking `current.interval` (usually 900 s) to normalize accumulated rain to mm/h. When “启用降雨影响” is on (the default), its bounded penalty is smoothly interpolated through 0 at 0.2 mm/h, -2 at 1, -6 at 3, and -10 at 8 mm/h or more; malformed or missing intervals are unknown. This removes hard jumps at rain-rate boundaries while keeping trace drizzle weak. Turning the option off removes the current rain penalty, yesterday-rain-to-sunny-day increment, rain gate on current cloud support, and all forecast rain suppression/gating; it leaves non-rain temperature, humidity, and cloud observations available. The current-day daily precipitation forecast is not used to suppress current risk because it can include rain that has not yet occurred; actual recent rain affects current risk, while hourly precipitation probability belongs to the outlook. Temperature uses a broad trapezoid (0 outside 5–22°C, ramps to +6 by 10°C, stays there through 18°C, then tapers to 0 at 22°C); humidity and cloud contributions are likewise bounded and continuous. These are cautious heuristics, not calibrated biological response curves. Yesterday's observed rain followed by a currently dry, warm, sunny day receives only +3 contextual points. A sunny streak is not treated as proof that pollen has been depleted. A Beijing-specific three-year study found warmer conditions and falling humidity associated with within-season pollen increases; hourly peaks varied by site/time, and only 0.1 mm/day on a peak day showed no significant daily-count effect. A two-year Nanjing study similarly found warm, low-humidity, low-rain peak conditions, but reported taxon- and lag-dependent temperature relationships, including cooler temperatures in the weeks before Cupressaceae flowering. These findings support cautious, stage-aware modifiers—not a universal fixed temperature response or numeric threshold ([Beijing Cupressaceae study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12019579/), [Nanjing pollen study](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018JD029026)). Wind effects are omitted from the generic score because local direction/source effects vary and higher wind may dilute concentrations. Relative humidity is read from the [Open-Meteo current/hourly weather API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs); its docs specify that current precipitation is a backward-looking sum and expose the aggregation interval.
- Missing, malformed, boolean, or non-finite weather fields are treated as unknown, not as zero rain or clear skies; sunny-weather bonuses require the needed observations to be present. The six-hour outlook selects timestamped observations from the actual next six local hours (not the first six hourly-array entries, which are often already in the past).
- Rain weights remain provisional. A Beijing Cupressaceae study reported no significant daily-count effect from only 0.1 mm of rain on a peak day, while a León event study that included Cupressaceae found rain-event scavenging varied by intensity (its low-intensity group was below 1 mm/h, with stronger average scavenging in the medium/high groups). These results support treating trace rain differently from sustained rain, but the climate/site difference means those percentages are not transferred into this household's score; thresholds require local pollen observations to calibrate ([Beijing study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12019579/), [León rain-event study poster](https://grupos.unileon.es/atmos/files/2022/11/2019_MEDPALYNO-Poster.pdf)).
- The six-hour outlook uses continuous bounded proxies rather than hard hourly cutoffs: warmth follows the current-temperature trapezoid; clear-sky support fades continuously with cloud cover and rain probability; low-humidity support fades from 50% toward 30%; and forecast rain suppresses the outlook gradually from 20% to 80% probability. Contributions are summed over only the timestamped next-six-hour samples and capped at ±6 points. Rain in the outlook can remove only this forecast uplift: it never reduces the current assessed risk or turns off a purifier on its own. The explanation reports both qualifying-hour counts and the numeric warm/clear, humidity, and rain components for audit.
- Weather is polled every 15 minutes and is ignored after 60 minutes, or if its fetch timestamp is missing or more than five minutes in the future. A failed refresh may retain the last payload for diagnostics, but the timestamp guard prevents stale weather from affecting the score.
- Each weather payload is bound to the requested coordinates and timezone. Changing home location/timezone clears the cached forecast; a response that finishes after a location change is discarded, and the scoring function independently rejects mismatched location metadata.
- Social/allergy chatter, after explicit operator review, adds 6/12/18 points for slight/clear/exceptional discussion. The maximum can prompt only the normal tier by itself, not high or extreme; it is a broad symptom/activity proxy, not a Cupressaceae measurement, and expires after 12 hours.
- Configured annual local season adds 4 monitoring points only. It never claims that pollen was measured.
- A verified official page or explicitly reviewed manual forecast that names a future peak is kept separate from today's concentration: it is shown in the explanation, but does not force the current tier high several days early. Negative wording such as “高峰尚未到来” is excluded from current-level classification while its date can still define the forecast window. Within the configured lead window it requests a one-tier pre-ramp; when the predicted peak window begins, its official level enters current scoring. Automatic page windows keep the final forecast day inclusive, with only a 12-hour post-window grace and a seven-day maximum publication age. Manual future windows must include start and end, be at most ten days long, and be reviewed within seven days.
- Official forecast periods expressed by旬 (for example “3月中下旬至4月上旬”) are parsed into an approximate calendar window. The estimate is explicitly marked in the UI; it is not presented as a precise peak date.
- Numeric forecast bands such as “3到4级” are interpreted at the lower endpoint (level 3), not matched as a standalone “4级”; a published range is not silently promoted to its most severe edge. The UI labels that interpretation separately from an explicit single level.
- The configured 17:10 window (40 minutes by default) raises the score to at least “normal.” If this schedule alone lifted a weak tier, it can end at its configured boundary; when pollen risk or the indoor-residual floor independently requires a higher tier, the ordinary 90-minute downshift dwell still applies.
- After high/extreme risk, a linear tail contributes up to 18 points over the configured indoor residual period (18 h by default). While that tail is active, it explicitly holds the target tier at least at normal even after its numeric score falls below the normal band; the timer is anchored to the last current/forecast high-risk sample, not to the hysteresis-delayed effective tier. This is an adjustable 0–72 h control hold, not a measured indoor-pollen clearance time. A Beijing 2019 observation reported that a ten-day high Cupressaceae peak was followed by low-to-moderate outdoor counts through the next 15 days until season end. A separate 10-month indoor study found cypress pollen's allergenic potency persisted in settled samples; that is not evidence of equivalent airborne concentration or a need to run purifiers at high speed for months. The tail balances this post-peak uncertainty while current local reports should be refreshed; settled dust still needs cleaning ([Beijing peak/post-peak observation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10128578/), [indoor cypress allergenic-potency study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24125158/)). Lower risk is accepted only after a 90-minute dwell.

Score bands are weak `<18`, normal `18–41`, high `42–67`, extreme `≥68`; weather/social/season alone are prevented from classifying extreme. Current score includes bounded weather suppression; the six-hour forecast tier may pre-ramp but is never allowed to lower today's tier. Upward transitions are immediate; downshifts wait 90 minutes. The outdoor pollen signal is not a direct indoor sensor, so results are recommendations, not medical advice.

## Fan target and physical actuation

Each selected purifier has a user-defined weak-pollen baseline (`off`, low, medium, high, auto). An `off` baseline is never automatically powered on. Explicit fan speeds scale only within low → medium → high; auto remains auto because its actual airflow is appliance-controlled and is not guaranteed to be higher. Normal strength permits at most one step; high strength follows the risk tier up to two steps; extra strength advances one risk band earlier (so a low baseline reaches high at the normal tier). All targets saturate at the physical high mode; “extra” does not invent a turbo setting. Weak returns to the configured baseline.

The room estimate uses 160 m² × 2.5 m = 400 m³. With 23 devices and the supplied *nominal airflow* of 20/70/150 m³/h per device, aggregate estimates are 460/1,610/3,450 m³/h, or approximately 1.15/4.03/8.63 nominal air-volume turnovers per hour. 15× and 20× would require 6,000 and 8,000 m³/h. These are not CADR: filter efficiency, installation, leakage, and actual airflow are not known, so the UI explicitly avoids presenting airflow turnover as certified clean-air delivery. In addition to this full-fleet theoretical range, the live panel estimates the configured, calibrated devices' target airflow at the current tier and separates the currently online subtotal. Automatic mode remains unrated rather than being assigned an invented number.

For safety, the controller never sends a burst of mode taps. It chooses no more than five devices per controller tick, prioritizes an explicitly requested manual target, then fairly orders remaining devices by oldest last-send time. Lamp-confirmation timeouts start only for that dispatched batch; devices waiting for a later batch are not treated as failed. Each device has at most one action in flight, which is durably checkpointed before MQTT publish; the controller then waits for command acknowledgement and the expected lamp transition before proceeding after a cooldown. If a mode does not change within 12 seconds after dispatch, it records the failure and retries cautiously; three consecutive failures pause that device until settings are saved again. If the service restarts while a physical pulse may be in flight, the result is treated as uncertain: it waits for a newer lamp report and never blindly replays that pulse. If no confirming state arrives, the device is paused for operator review. Weather payloads are intentionally ephemeral, so startup forces an immediate weather refresh instead of trusting a persisted fetch timestamp for data that was not persisted. The 90-minute downshift dwell also restarts from the first post-restart observation; time while the controller was stopped is not counted as continuous low-risk evidence, and the active timer uses a monotonic clock so operating-system clock corrections cannot satisfy or prolong it. A manually issued MQTT command or an unexpected physical/other-controller lamp transition pauses automatic actions for that purifier for 30 minutes; expected transitions from the currently pending smart command are not mistaken for manual takeover. Power changes use the GPIO5 momentary pulse (300 ms), not a held optocoupler output.

## Settings required

1. Enter home coordinates and timezone for local weather context.
2. Enter the locality's cypress pollen season, current official level, source URL, and any separately predicted future level/window. Explicitly mark a manual signal reviewed when rechecking the same level; ordinary saves do not extend its freshness.
3. Optionally enter a verified trend level and short evidence note from Baidu Index or public allergy reports; do not put account credentials here.
4. Set each commissioned purifier's weak-season baseline. Only calibrated devices can be selected for automatic control.
5. Choose normal/high/extra aggressiveness, review the capacity estimate, and decide whether the default-on rain influence matches the household's preference before enabling.

## Validation and remaining integration

`test_smart_control.py` covers room-volume arithmetic, selected-device target airflow, calibrated/online capacity separation, location gating, evidence caps, continuous current and six-hour forecast weather modifiers, automatic and manual peak staging, Chinese GBK/GB18030 forecast-page decoding, target speed limits, government URL restrictions, fresh/old/missing Cupressaceae evidence, one-step physical actuation, 23-device fair dispatch batches, restart reconciliation of non-idempotent pulses, and post-restart observation-clock recovery. For the currently configured example (160 m² × 2.5 m, 23 units), rated airflow at high totals 3,450 m³/h, or 8.63 nominal air changes/hour. That is below a 15×/20× comparison target even before accounting for filter efficiency; actual CADR cannot be inferred without device test data. Firmware must be rebuilt and exercised on a bench before smart-control enablement on a real purifier. Baidu Index's official help page says it does not provide an open API; the current implementation intentionally accepts operator-verified social levels instead of scraping logged-in or unstable pages.
